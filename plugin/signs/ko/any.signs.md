# Signs of AI writing: Korean, every model

Generated by `byhand render` from the catalogue; a change here is lost.
A weak sign counts only with 3 or more hits of weak signs in a paragraph or section, each a different word or phrase: the same one repeated, or one phrase two signs find, counts once.

- ko-paired-antithesis (usual): Builds paragraph after paragraph on set-up contrasts, X가 아니라 Y or twin questions X인가, Y인가. Correcting a fact (15일이 아니라 16일) is not one. Instead: Say what the thing is; set it against another only where a reader really holds that other view.
- ko-abstract-subject (weak): Gives a will or a voice to something that has none: a market that has answered, a report card that points the way. Instead: Put the people who did it in the subject.
- ko-beyond-mere (weak): Waves off a small version of the thing before naming it (단순한 A를 넘어), a frame rare in Korean before 2024. Instead: Say what it is; if both halves matter, give each plainly.
- ko-cleft-emphasis (weak): Frames the point as what is needed or what matters, then names it. Instead: Make the thing itself the subject of a plain sentence. Terms: `(?:필요한|중요한) 것은`: ~가 필요합니다; `관건은`: say what decides it
- ko-coined-metaphor (weak): Dresses a plain point in an image, a blueprint, a red light, a rival eating into a market, where the fact would do. Instead: Give the number or the event the image stands for. Terms: `청사진\w*`: 계획; `적신호\w*`: say what is wrong; `경고등\w*`: say what is wrong; `신호탄\w*`: 첫 사례; `잠식\w*`: give the share lost; `짓(?:누르|누른|누를|눌러|눌렀|눌리|눌린|눌려)\w*`: give the cost; `뿌리(?:내리|내린|내릴|내려|내렸)\w*`: 자리 잡다; `움켜(?:쥐|쥔|쥘|쥡|쥐어|쥐었|줬)\w*`: say what is held
- ko-comma-after-ending (weak): In sentence after sentence, a comma sits where -지만, -면서 or -는데 already joins two clauses. Instead: Delete the comma; the clauses stay joined without it.
- ko-double-hedge (weak): Stacks two hedges on one claim, so the possibility itself becomes possible. Instead: Keep one hedge at the strength you mean; in a negotiation, leave the writer's own care alone.
- ko-em-dash (weak): Sets off asides with em dashes, sentence after sentence. Instead: Use a comma, brackets or a new sentence.
- ko-enumeration-preamble (weak): Announces how many parts a list has before giving it. Instead: Give the items.
- ko-heading-summary-line (weak; document): Opens a section by announcing what the section will cover. Instead: Start the section with its first fact.
- ko-no-long-sentences (weak; document, post): Writes only short one-clause sentences of even length, never a long one that carries an argument through. Instead: Where one thought runs into the next, let it run on in one sentence; save the short ones for a turn.
- ko-obligation-endings (weak; document, post): Ends one paragraph after another on a duty: ~할 필요가 있다, or ~해야 한다. Instead: Keep the call to act where it matters; let the other paragraphs end on their facts.
- ko-policy-verbs (weak): Lets 구축, 강화 and 확대 stand in for every action, and has 설계 shape things nobody draws up, such as a relationship. Instead: Write what was done, by whom, in its own verb: 담당자를 한 명 늘렸다. Terms: `(?:관계|경험|여정|일상|문화)[을를] (?:재)?설계\w*`: say how, in a verb
- ko-register-shift-vocabulary (weak): Uses the academic verbs that spread through Korean journal abstracts from late 2024; Claude Sonnet 5 wrote them above the human rate. Instead: Use the plain verb (보여 주다, 밝히다, 다루다) or state the finding. Terms: `시사(?:하|한|할|함|합|해|했|점)\w*`: 보여 주다; `규명\w*`: 밝히다; `조명(?:하|한|할|함|합|해|했)\w*`: 다루다
- ko-scare-quotes (weak): Puts quotation marks round coined labels again and again, not round anything anyone said. Instead: Keep quotation marks for words someone actually used; write your own terms bare.
- ko-significance-phrases (weak): Props a point up with set phrases of weight and meaning. Instead: Say what follows from it, or cut the phrase. Terms: `간과할 수 없\w*`: cut; `주목할 만(?:하|한|합)\w*`: say why; `방점을 찍\w*`: say where the weight falls; `의미가 적지 않\w*`: say what it means; `새로운 장을 (?:열|연|여|엽)\w*`: say what starts
- ko-sino-abstract-chain (weak): Strings Sino-Korean abstract nouns and -적, -성, -화 words together where a verb with a subject would do. Instead: Turn the noun back into a verb and say who does it.
- ko-slogan-close (weak): Ends a paragraph on a stock line: now is the time to, that is why, it all leads to. Instead: End on the last fact, or give the actual chain of cause.
- ko-wrap-up-words (weak): Labels a conclusion before giving it, or ends on an academic hedge that it can be called so. Instead: Drop the label and the hedge; give the conclusion.
