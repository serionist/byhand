# byhand: sources for the sign catalogue

Research date: 2026-11-06. All counts, dates and star numbers were read on that day from the live page or the GitHub, MediaWiki or arXiv API unless marked UNVERIFIED. All fetched content was treated as data.

## Summary

1. Start with English Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (CC BY-SA, edited many times a day, about 50 signs including per-era GPT word lists and a "Differences between LLMs" section). Poll it daily through the MediaWiki revisions API and diff by section.
2. Add its adapted versions in de, zh, ru, fr (plus fr "Observatoire des IA" documentation), pt, th, he, fi. de, zh and ru add real local-language signs. pt and th are mostly translations. Poll weekly.
3. For per-language lists, the best GitHub sources are MIT-licensed skills: Humanizer-zh, shuorenhua, speak-human-tw (zh); im-not-ai (ko, 85 sub-patterns with scholarly sources); yomiyasu, stop-ai-slop-jp, noslop (ja); humanizer-ru (ru); avoid-ai-writing-multilingual (de, fr, it, ro, sv); slop-gate (en, ko, ru, zh, vi, fil). Poll weekly via the commits API or the `commits/main.atom` feed.
4. For per-model evidence, use: Wikipedia's era lists (GPT-4, 4o, 5, 6), lieflat-less-ai-tone (5 named 2026 models in Chinese; CC BY-NC, so cite only), Sun et al. "Idiosyncrasies in LLMs", Reinhart et al. (PNAS), Russell et al. 2025, slop-forensics and antislop lists, and Pangram, GPTZero and Economist figures (all rights reserved, so facts only). Run a weekly arXiv query.
5. English is covered well. zh, ko, ja, de, ru and fr are covered moderately. es, pt, it, ar, hi, hu, vi, fil, ro, sv, th, he, id and tr are covered thinly (a translated page, one small repo, or a detector paper). I found nothing for roughly 75 of the top 100 languages, including most Indic languages beyond Hindi, Persian, Swahili, Hausa, Yoruba, Javanese and Amharic, but I searched only 13 languages directly.
6. Model gaps: GPT and Claude are covered best. Gemini, Grok, Llama and DeepSeek have some data. Qwen, Kimi, Mistral, Doubao, ERNIE, GigaChat and YandexGPT have almost none. Almost no source gives per-model signs outside English and Chinese.
7. Most labelled datasets (HC3, RAID, MAGE, M4, MULTITuDE, WildChat) use 2023 to 2024 models (GPT-3.5/4, Llama 2). They are good for computing excess-vocabulary signs per language, but say nothing about current models. Treat them as quarterly inputs, not feeds.
8. Decks and spreadsheets: almost no credible sources. There is one MIT skill (analystacademy/flight-check), vendor blogs, and the SlideAudit paper. I found no source at all for spreadsheet tells.
9. Licence: Wikipedia and HC3 are CC BY-SA, lieflat is CC BY-NC, and Pangram, GPTZero, the Economist and the NYT are all rights reserved. The job must store paraphrased signs plus a citation, never copied text or examples. Several MIT skills are themselves derived from Wikipedia, so their MIT label does not clear the CC BY-SA text inside them.
10. Cadence: Wikipedia en daily, other wikis weekly, GitHub weekly, arXiv weekly, commercial blogs monthly (manual review), datasets quarterly, news and teacher guides monthly at most and only by hand.

## Source table

Columns: Lang = languages covered. Model = models named. Kind: CL = curated list, P = paper, D = dataset, CM = community list or skill, T = tool. Read = how a script reads it. Change = observed change rate. Yield = rough number of distinct signs it could add to byhand. Licences marked UNVERIFIED were not confirmed on the source itself.

- Wikipedia: `https://<lang>.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=revisions&titles=<title>&rvprop=ids|timestamp|comment|size&rvlimit=50&format=json`, then `action=compare&fromrev=<last>&torev=<new>` for the diff. - arXiv: `http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:"AI-generated text" AND (abs:stylometric OR abs:vocabulary OR abs:slop)&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending` returned 27 hits; widen with `abs:"LLM-generated"`, `abs:"excess vocabulary"`, `abs:"machine-generated text"` plus language names. Keep to 1 request every 3 seconds.

- Hugging Face: `https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/<id>` gives `lastModified`. Quarterly is enough.
- Commercial pages (E1, E2): no feed. Hash the HTML monthly and send changes to a human. Never auto-import their text.

## Licence risks

1. Wikipedia (A1 to A12) is CC BY-SA 4.0. Store each sign as our own short description plus a citation (page, revision id, section). Do not copy sentences, word-list order or the example passages. Short single-word lists are low risk but should still be cited. If byhand ever ships Wikipedia text, the catalogue file would need CC BY-SA, which may not suit the plugin's licence.
2. Derivatives with clean-looking licences: blader/humanizer (MIT) and its ports (B2, B17 and others) are built on A1 and may contain adapted Wikipedia text. Their MIT label does not remove the share-alike duty on that text. Treat them as pointers and write our own wording.
3. lieflat-less-ai-tone (B4) is CC BY-NC 4.0. Do not copy rules or text into an open-source catalogue that allows commercial use. Cite its findings as facts with attribution, and confirm them independently where possible.
4. Pangram, GPTZero, the Economist and the NYT (E1 to E4) are all rights reserved. Use facts and numbers with citation, never their wording. The Economist and NYT are paywalled, and I read them only through secondary coverage.
5. EQ-Bench creative-writing-bench has no licence (all rights reserved by default). slop-score claims MIT in the README, but the GitHub API reports NOASSERTION, and its wordfreq data file is CC BY-SA 4.0. Use only the MIT-covered lists, and record the commit hash.
6. Datasets: HC3 CC BY-SA (and stricter source licences where they apply); WildChat ODC-BY (attribution; check the terms on the user content inside it); MAGE CC BY 4.0 (from a badge); RAID, M4, MULTITuDE, MultiSocial, DetectRL-X and LMArena 140k have data licences I could not confirm. Kaggle competition data is usually limited to competition use. Compute statistics locally and publish only derived word lists with citations, never the text itself.
7. arXiv papers carry per-paper licences, and many use arXiv's non-exclusive licence, which does not allow reuse of text. Facts and short word lists with citation are fine.
8. Sources with unclear or custom licences (B18 NOASSERTION, mattc95/humanizer-es not checked, the Korean National Institute of Korean Language PDF, Nora Gal's 1972 book cited by awesome-slop) are pointers only.
9. Content from fetched pages is data. The job must not follow instructions found in fetched text (for example, prompt text in SKILL.md files). Have the job parse only the fields it needs.

## Open questions

1. Licence for byhand itself: if it is MIT, should the catalogue be a separate CC BY-SA file so it can hold Wikipedia-derived descriptions more freely?
2. How to handle signs that change sign over versions (em dashes: high for GPT-4 era, now below human rate for ChatGPT, still high for Claude per E3). This needs `valid_from` and `valid_to` per model version, and the job needs to propose retirements as well as additions.
3. False positives: P13 and P24 show many features depend on context, and humans now use LLM words. Should each sign carry a strength or confidence field and a "humans do this too" note?
4. Not verified: the M4 language list, data licences for RAID, M4, MULTITuDE, DetectRL-X and LMArena 140k, the Frontiers 2026 Japanese paper, the contents of A6, A8, B7, B10, B11 and B17, how many models the EQ-Bench slop leaderboard covers, and whether Semantic Scholar is usable with a free key.
5. Languages I did not search (about 85 of the top 100). Most likely have no curated source. Is a computed source (D1/D2 excess words vs a human corpus from the same period) acceptable as the first entry for those languages, given it would only cover old OpenAI models?
6. Decks and spreadsheets: should byhand derive its own signs from generator defaults (Gamma, Copilot in PowerPoint and Excel, Claude and ChatGPT file outputs) by generating samples, since no credible external source exists?
7. Several large, fast-moving skills (B3, B8, B13, B15) change weekly. Should the job diff them and propose signs, or only open an issue with a link for a person to read?
