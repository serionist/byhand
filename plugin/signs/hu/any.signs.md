# Signs of AI writing: Hungarian, every model

Generated by `byhand render` from the catalogue; a change here is lost.
A weak sign counts only with 3 or more hits of weak signs in a paragraph or section, each a different word or phrase: the same one repeated, or one phrase two signs find, counts once.

- hu-address-switching (usual): Changes how it speaks to the reader partway through: te in one sentence, Ön in the next, or ön and Ön mixed. Instead: Settle on te or Ön for this reader before writing, and use that form in every sentence.
- hu-ai-vocabulary (weak): Reaches for weighty abstract words where a plain word or the fact itself would do. Instead: Say what the thing does or why it matters, in the everyday word. Terms: `(?:leg)?zökkenőmentes\w*`: simán, gond nélkül; `kulcsfontosság\w*`: fontos, and for whom; `(?:leg)?elengedhetetlen\w*`: kell, nem megy nélküle; `(?:leg)?mélyreható\w*`: alapos; `(?:leg)?átfogó\w*`: teljes, or name what it covers; `kulcsszerep\w*`: say what it does; `nélkülözhetetlen\w*`: kell; `aspektus\w*`: szempont, oldal; `perspektív\w*`: szempont, kilátás
- hu-calque-phrases (weak): Builds on English frames in Hungarian words: helps to do, makes it possible, including, serves as. Instead: Use the Hungarian construction: the verb itself, -hat/-het, a …-t is, or no verb for 'is'. Terms: `\w+ként\s+szolgál(?:nak|hat|hatnak)?`: X a Y, or arra való; `beleértve`: a …-t is, köztük; `lehetővé\s+t(?:esz|eszi|eszik|ett|ette|ették|eheti|ehetik|éve|enné|enni)`: így … tud, or -hat/-het; `segít(?:het|enek|hetnek|ett|ettek)?\s+(?:az?\s+\w+\s+)?(?:megelőzni|javítani|optimalizálni|csökkenteni|megőrizni|fenntartani|erősíteni|előmozdítani|felismerni|növelni|fejleszteni|biztosítani|megérteni|minimalizálni|kezelni)`: the verb itself: csökkenti, kevesebb lesz
- hu-em-dash (weak): Sets off a clause with the long English dash (—), which Hungarian typography doesn't use. Instead: Use the spaced short dash (–), a comma or brackets, or end the sentence.
- hu-english-quotes (weak): Quotes with English curly marks (“…”); Hungarian opens low and closes high („…”), and plain text uses straight quotes. Instead: Write „…”, or straight quotes where the tool gives them.
- hu-explicit-subject-repeat (weak; document, email): Names the same subject at the head of sentence after sentence, though Hungarian leaves out a subject that carries on: it reads as translated. Instead: Drop the repeated subject where the verb already carries it, or join the sentences; a pronoun can stand once. Don't trade the noun for synonyms.
- hu-parenthetical-gloss (weak; document, email): Puts a term's translation in brackets beside it, English after Hungarian or the reverse, for a reader who knows the word. Instead: Use the word the team uses, once, with no gloss.
- hu-promo-adjectives (weak): Praises a place, an event or a product with stock words of awe, as a brochure does. Instead: Say what is there or what happened; one detail does more than the adjective. Terms: `felejthetetlen\w*`: what you will remember; `magával ragad\w*`: what held you; `varázslatos\w*`: what it looks like; `(?:leg)?lenyűgöz\w*`: the detail that impressed you
- hu-title-case-headings (weak; document, deck, post): Capitalises every word of a heading or a bold label, as English titles do; Hungarian capitalises the first word and proper names only. Instead: Keep the capital on the first word and on names: Negyedéves bérköltség és létszám.
