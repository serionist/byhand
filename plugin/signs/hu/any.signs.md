# Signs of AI writing: Hungarian, every model

Generated by `byhand render` from the catalogue; a change here is lost.
A weak sign counts only with 3 or more hits of weak signs in a paragraph or section, each a different word or phrase: the same one repeated, or one phrase two signs find, counts once.

- hu-address-switching (usual): Changes how it speaks to the reader partway through one text: Ön and ön mixed, or Ön and te. Instead: Keep the form the text opens with; where it is unclear, take the one the reader last used with you.
- hu-ai-vocabulary (weak): Reaches for weighty abstract words where a plain word or the fact itself would do. Instead: Say what the thing does or why it matters, in the everyday word. Terms: `aspektus\w*`: oldal, szempont; `átfogó\w*`: teljes, or name what it covers; `elengedhetetlen\w*`: kell, nem megy nélküle; `kulcsfontosság\w*`: fontos, and for whom; `kulcsszerep\w*`: say what it does; `mélyreható\w*`: alapos; `nélkülözhetetlen\w*`: kell; `perspektív\w*`: kilátás, szempont; `zökkenőmentes\w*`: gond nélkül, simán
- hu-calque-phrases (weak): Builds on English frames in Hungarian words: helps to do, including, makes it possible, serves as. Instead: Use Hungarian's own: is after the noun for including, no verb for 'is', the bare verb, or the verb with -hat/-het for 'can'. Terms: `\w+ként\s+szolgál(?:hat|hatnak|nak)?`: arra való, or X a Y; `beleértve`: a …-t is, köztük; `lehetővé\s+t(?:eheti|ehetik|enné|enni|esz|eszi|eszik|ett|ette|ették|éve)`: a -hat/-het ending, or így … tud; `segít(?:enek|ett|ettek|het|hetnek)?\s+(?:az?\s+\w+\s+)?(?:biztosítani|csökkenteni|előmozdítani|erősíteni|fejleszteni|felismerni|fenntartani|javítani|kezelni|megelőzni|megérteni|megőrizni|minimalizálni|növelni|optimalizálni)`: the verb itself: csökkenti, kevesebb lesz
- hu-em-dash (weak): Sets off a clause with the long English dash (—), which Hungarian typography doesn't use. Instead: Use brackets, commas, full stops or the spaced short dash (–).
- hu-english-quotes (weak): Quotes with English curly marks (“…”); Hungarian opens low and closes high („…”), and plain text uses straight quotes. Instead: Write „…”, or straight quotes where the tool gives them.
- hu-explicit-subject-repeat (weak; document, email): Spells out a subject the verb's ending already names (én, mi, ő) or a noun nothing has displaced, sentence after sentence, as English must; it reads as translated. Instead: Any of: a pronoun once; leaving out the subject the verb's ending carries; one sentence where two share it. Never a synonym in its place.
- hu-parenthetical-gloss (weak; document, email): Puts a term's translation in brackets beside it, English after Hungarian or the reverse, for a reader who knows the word. Instead: Use the word the team uses, once, with no gloss.
- hu-promo-adjectives (weak): Praises an event, a place or a product with stock words of awe, as a brochure does. Instead: Name the dish, the feature or the view the reader would meet there, and let them judge it. Terms: `felejthetetlen\w*`: what you will remember; `lenyűgöz\w*`: the detail that impressed you; `magával ragad\w*`: what held you; `varázslatos\w*`: what it looks like
- hu-tacked-on-participle (weak): Tacks a -va/-ve participle onto a finished sentence after a comma, to say what the fact secures, serves or reflects. Instead: End on the fact; if what it secures matters, give that its own sentence and its own subject.
- hu-title-case-headings (weak; document, deck, post): Capitalises every word of a bold label or a heading, as English titles do; Hungarian capitalises only proper names and the first word. Instead: Capitalise names and the first word: **Szabadságolási rend decemberre:**
