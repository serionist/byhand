# Signs of AI writing: every language, every model, to check a deck or a sheet

Generated by `byhand render` from the catalogue; a change here is lost.
A weak sign counts only with 3 or more hits of weak signs in a paragraph or section, each a different word or phrase, even two from one sign: the same one repeated, a mark such as a dash, quotes or bold however often it appears and whatever words it holds, or one phrase two signs find, counts once.

- any-label-above-title (usual; deck): Sets a kicker over slide after slide's title: a short label, often in capitals or, in another language's deck, in English, naming the topic the title then states. Instead: Open each slide with its title; mark a new part of the talk with a divider slide, not a label on every slide.
- any-takeaway-bar (usual; deck): Ends slide after slide on a takeaway box or a bar along the bottom that says again what the title or the body has just said. Instead: Let the title carry the point; keep a last line only for something new: a number, a decision, or what the next slide answers.
- any-colon-titles (weak; deck): Builds title after title on one frame: a topic, a colon, then a catchline. Instead: Write each title as the point its slide makes, in whatever shape that point takes.
- any-formula-separators (weak; sheet): Writes a formula as English Excel takes it, commas between the arguments, in a sheet whose Excel takes semicolons. Instead: Use the sheet's own separator, a semicolon in French or Portuguese Excel, unless the user works in an English Excel.
- any-invented-figures (weak; deck, sheet): Puts figures nobody gave it on a slide or in a sheet: round percentages and multiples with no source, or sample rows made up to fill a table. Instead: Use the user's figures and say where each comes from; where one is missing, leave the cell empty or name it for the user to fill.
