# Signs of AI writing: Korean, every model

Generated by `byhand render` from the catalogue; a change here is lost.
A weak sign counts only with 3 or more hits of weak signs in a paragraph or section, each a different word or phrase: the same one repeated, or one phrase two signs find, counts once.

- ko-beyond-mere (usual): Raises a subject a rank with 단순한 X를 넘어, a frame that was rare in Korean before 2024. Instead: Say what it is; if both halves matter, give each plainly.
- ko-paired-antithesis (usual): Runs paragraph after paragraph on paired contrasts: twin questions (X인가, Y인가) or denying one thing to assert another (X가 아니라 Y). Instead: Keep one contrast where it carries the point; say the rest straight.
- ko-abstract-subject (weak): Makes an idea, a number or an era the one who asks, proves or calls. Instead: Name the person or team that acts.
- ko-cleft-emphasis (weak): Frames the point as what is needed or what matters, then names it. Instead: Make the thing itself the subject of a plain sentence. Terms: `(?:필요한|중요한) 것은`: ~가 필요합니다; `관건은`: say what decides it
- ko-coined-metaphor (weak): Lays fresh metaphors of weight, decay or warning signals over a plain point, or judges an analysis with a feeling word. Instead: Say the literal claim: who loses what, and by how much. Terms: `청사진\w*`: 계획; `적신호\w*`: say what is wrong; `경고등\w*`: say what is wrong; `신호탄\w*`: 첫 사례; `잠식\w*`: say who loses what; `짓(?:누르|누른|누를|눌러|눌렀|눌리|눌린|눌려)\w*`: 부담이 크다; `뿌리(?:내리|내린|내릴|내려|내렸)\w*`: 자리 잡다; `움켜(?:쥐|쥔|쥘|쥡|쥐어|쥐었|줬)\w*`: 갖다
- ko-comma-after-ending (weak): Puts a comma straight after a linking ending (-지만, -면서, -는데), and a comma in most sentences. Instead: Let the ending mark the pause; drop the comma.
- ko-double-hedge (weak): Stacks two hedges on one claim, so the possibility itself becomes possible. Instead: Keep one hedge at the strength you mean; in a negotiation, leave the writer's own care alone.
- ko-em-dash (weak): Sets off asides with em dashes, sentence after sentence. Instead: Use a comma, brackets or a new sentence.
- ko-enumeration-preamble (weak): Announces how many parts a list has before giving it. Instead: Give the items.
- ko-heading-summary-line (weak; document): Opens a section by announcing what the section will cover. Instead: Start the section with its first fact.
- ko-no-long-sentences (weak; document, post): Writes only short one-clause sentences of even length, never a long one that carries an argument through. Instead: Join neighbouring sentences with a linking ending (-고, -는데, -면서) where the thought runs on.
- ko-obligation-endings (weak; document, post): Ends one paragraph after another on a duty: ~할 필요가 있다, or ~해야 한다. Instead: Keep the call to act where it matters; let the other paragraphs end on their facts.
- ko-policy-verbs (weak): Falls back on a few all-purpose verbs (확대, 강화, 구축) and designs abstract things such as relationships or experiences. Instead: Name the concrete act: who opens, adds, hires or meets whom. Terms: `(?:관계|경험|여정|일상|문화)[을를] (?:재)?설계\w*`: 만들다, 맺다
- ko-register-shift-vocabulary (weak): Uses the academic verbs that spread through Korean writing from late 2024, which models of 2026 write above the human rate. Instead: Use the plain verb (보여 주다, 밝히다) or state the finding. Terms: `시사(?:하|한|할|함|합|해|했|점)\w*`: 보여 주다; `규명\w*`: 밝히다
- ko-scare-quotes (weak): Puts quotation marks round coined labels again and again, not round anything anyone said. Instead: Quote only what someone said or wrote; use the plain word for the rest.
- ko-significance-phrases (weak): Props a point up with set phrases of weight and meaning. Instead: Say what follows from it, or cut the phrase. Terms: `간과할 수 없\w*`: cut; `주목할 만(?:하|한|합)\w*`: say why; `시사하는 바가 (?:크|큽|컸)\w*`: say what it implies; `방점을 찍\w*`: say where the weight falls; `의미가 적지 않\w*`: say what it means; `새로운 장을 (?:열|연|여|엽)\w*`: say what starts
- ko-sino-abstract-chain (weak): Strings Sino-Korean abstract nouns and -적, -성, -화 words together where a verb with a subject would do. Instead: Turn the noun back into a verb and say who does it.
- ko-slogan-close (weak): Ends a paragraph on a stock line: now is the time to, that is why, it all leads to. Instead: End on the last fact, or give the actual chain of cause.
- ko-wrap-up-words (weak): Labels a conclusion before giving it, or ends on an academic hedge that it can be called so. Instead: Drop the label and the hedge; give the conclusion.
