# Signs of AI writing: Hungarian, every model, to check a text

Generated by `byhand render` from the catalogue; a change here is lost.
A weak sign counts only with 3 or more hits of weak signs in a paragraph or section, each a different word or phrase, even two from one sign: the same one repeated, a mark such as a dash, quotes or bold however often it appears and whatever words it holds, or one phrase two signs find, counts once.
A sign holds for the versions it names: `5.x` is the whole 5 series, `and later` every newer one.

- hu-address-switching (usual): Changes how it speaks to the reader partway through one text: Ön and ön mixed, or Ön and te. Instead: Keep the form the text opens with; where it is unclear, take the one the reader last used with you.
- hu-hyphenated-case-ending (usual; gemini 2.5 and later; grok 4 and later; llama 3.3 and later; mistral 3.1 and later; qwen 3 and later): Joins a case ending to a Hungarian name that ends in a or e with a hyphen and no long vowel (Anna-val), which a native speaker doesn't write. Instead: Join the ending to the name and lengthen its last vowel: Annával, Bencének, Nyíregyházán.
- hu-place-name-suffix (usual; gemini 2.5 and later; grok 4 and later; llama 3.3 and later; mistral 3.1 and later; qwen 3 and later): Says a thing is in a town that takes -en, -on or -ön with -ban or -ben instead (Szegedben for Szegeden), which a native speaker doesn't write. Instead: Use the town's own ending: Budapesten, Kecskeméten, Miskolcon, Pécsen, Szegeden. Leave alone what is right: a club's, a county's or a hotel's name (Hilton Budapestben, Jász-Nagykun-Szolnokban), a verb that takes -ban (csalódtam Budapestben), and the town's other endings (Győrön keresztül).
- hu-ai-vocabulary (weak): Reaches for weighty abstract words where a plain word or the fact itself would do. Instead: Use the everyday word, or say what it does or why it matters. Words: aspektus…: oldal, szempont; átfogó…: teljes, or name what it covers; elengedhetetlen…: kell, nem megy nélküle; kulcsfontosság…: fontos, and for whom; kulcsszerep…: say what it does; mélyreható…: alapos; nélkülözhetetlen…: kell; perspektív…: kilátás, szempont; zökkenőmentes…: gond nélkül, simán
- hu-calque-phrases (weak): Builds on English frames in Hungarian words: helps to do, including, makes it possible, serves as. Instead: Use Hungarian's own: is after the noun for including, no verb for 'is', the bare verb, or the verb with -hat/-het for 'can'. Words: …ként szolgál: arra való, or X a Y; beleértve: a …-t is, köztük; lehetővé tesz: a -hat/-het ending, or így … tud; segít kezelni: the verb itself: csökkenti, kevesebb lesz
- hu-em-dash (weak): Sets off a clause with the long English dash (—), which Hungarian typography doesn't use. Instead: Use brackets, commas, full stops or the spaced short dash (–).
- hu-english-quotes (weak): Quotes with English curly marks (“…”); Hungarian opens low and closes high („…”), and plain text uses straight quotes. Instead: Write „…”, or straight quotes where the tool gives them.
- hu-explicit-subject-repeat (weak; document, email, deck): Spells out a subject the verb's ending already names (én, mi, ő) or a noun nothing has displaced, sentence after sentence, as English must; it reads as translated. Instead: Any of: a pronoun once; leaving out the subject the verb's ending carries; one sentence where two share it. Never a synonym in its place.
- hu-parenthetical-gloss (weak; document, email, deck): Puts a term's translation in brackets beside it, English after Hungarian or the reverse, for a reader who knows the word. Instead: Use the word the team uses, once, with no gloss.
- hu-promo-adjectives (weak): Praises an event, a place or a product with stock words of awe, as a brochure does. Instead: Name the dish, the feature or the view the reader would meet there, and let them judge it. Words: felejthetetlen…: what you will remember; lenyűgöz…: the detail that impressed you; magával ragad…: what held you; varázslatos…: what it looks like
- hu-tacked-on-participle (weak): Tacks a -va/-ve participle onto a finished sentence after a comma, to say what the fact secures, serves or reflects. Instead: End on the fact; if what it secures matters, give that its own sentence and its own subject.
- hu-title-case-headings (weak; document, deck, post): Capitalises every word of a bold label or a heading, as English titles do; Hungarian capitalises only proper names and the first word. Instead: Capitalise names and the first word: **Szabadságolási rend decemberre:**
